Technical Project

Portal Clone

A Portal-style prototype built in a custom C++ engine with portal rendering, teleportation, puzzles, turret AI, and portal-aware collision.

The project focuses on the systems behind convincing portal gameplay: rendering a second space through a portal surface, transforming cameras and actors between linked portals, and keeping gameplay state readable as objects, projectiles, lasers, and AI interact with portals.

What I Learned

  • Transforming cameras, actors, vectors, and projectiles between linked portal spaces.
  • Separating portal view rendering from normal world rendering while keeping the result spatially convincing.
  • Extending an actor/component engine with first-person controls, quaternion transforms, and JSON level data.
  • Designing collision rules for portals, puzzle actors, lasers, turrets, and player movement.
  • Building interruptible AI behavior with state machines and portal-specific transitions.
  • Cleaning up level state reliably for reloads, input replay, sounds, colliders, and portal references.

01

Portal Rendering & Teleportation

The portal camera preserves the player's relative transform between two linked portal spaces, making the view through a portal feel continuous instead of like a disconnected render target.

Crossing logic distinguishes between looking through a portal, approaching its plane, and actually passing through it so teleportation does not jitter or repeatedly trigger.

  • Rendered a linked portal view using a secondary transformed camera.
  • Converted camera position and orientation from one portal's local space into the other's.
  • Used quaternion support for smoother first-person camera rotation.
  • Detected when the player crossed the portal plane instead of merely touching it.
  • Handled teleport cooldown/edge cases to avoid repeated crossing triggers.

02

Energy Puzzle Actors

I built puzzle objects around energy launchers, pellets, catchers, doors, and level reset behavior. These systems needed to cooperate with the portal system so puzzle objects could move through portal spaces cleanly.

The level reload path had to clear actors, colliders, portal links, temporary sounds, and input playback so repeated test runs behaved consistently.

  • Implemented energy launchers that spawn moving puzzle projectiles.
  • Added energy catchers that detect successful pellet delivery.
  • Connected catcher state to door activation and puzzle progression.
  • Supported level reset behavior for falling, failed runs, and replay testing.
  • Kept puzzle actors compatible with portal movement and collision checks.

03

Turret AI State Machine

Turrets transition between idle, priming, firing, search, falling, and dead states. Target acquisition and target loss drive combat behavior, while portal collision can interrupt any active behavior state.

That interruption rule kept displacement logic centralized instead of scattering portal checks across every AI branch.

  • Created idle, priming, firing, search, falling, and dead behavior states.
  • Transitioned from idle to priming when a target is acquired.
  • Required the target to remain visible before firing.
  • Moved into search when the target is lost and returned to idle after a delay.
  • Allowed portal collision to interrupt any non-dead state and force falling behavior.

04

State Diagram as Design Tool

The diagram makes the AI's behavior rules explicit: turrets prime before firing, search before returning to idle, fall after portal collision, and die when health reaches zero.

Including this makes the page less like a clip reel and more like an engineering breakdown, which is exactly what this technical project should be doing.

  • Mapped every turret state before implementing the behavior logic.
  • Defined transition conditions for target acquisition, target loss, falling, and death.
  • Separated health-driven transitions from vision-driven targeting transitions.
  • Used the diagram to reason about portal interruptions from any active state.

05

Lasers, Damage & Health

Laser segments use collision queries to damage actors, and the system supports laser behavior through portals. Health callbacks connect damage to turret death, player death, audio, and on-screen feedback.

This tied together rendering, collision, portals, and gameplay feedback into one testable interaction chain.

  • Built laser segments using collision checks against level actors.
  • Allowed lasers to continue through portals by transforming their direction and origin.
  • Added health components for player and turret damage handling.
  • Connected death behavior to callbacks instead of hard-coding it into the laser system.
  • Added damage indicators and audio feedback for clearer player response.

Interesting Challenges

Spatial Continuity

The portal camera and teleport transforms had to agree perfectly. If the view through the portal and the crossing transform disagree, the illusion breaks immediately.

State Cleanup

Level reloads had to cleanly reset actors, colliders, portals, sounds, and input replay so debugging did not inherit stale state from the previous run.
