Core Systems

01

Looping Environment & Escalator

The central horror trick was a seamless descent where the space repeats in a way that feels consistent, but wrong.

  • Used triggers to transition between repeated environment segments.
  • Aligned player and camera state to preserve orientation.
  • Handled backtracking and edge cases safely.
  • Checked lighting and audio continuity across loops.
Level Systems Triggers Polish

02

First-Person Interaction & Pickup

Exploration needed simple, reliable interactions so the player could read objects and progression without breaking tension.

  • Implemented pickup triggers, prompts, and feedback.
  • Connected collected objectives to game state.
  • Kept interactions safe across multiple areas.
  • Tuned clarity and pacing through playtests.
Gameplay Systems Interaction UI

03

Enemy Avoidance & Encounter Logic

Encounters had to feel tense and unpredictable without confusing players or trapping them in repeated spaces.

  • Built detection and proximity triggers for encounter beats.
  • Supported safe-zone and escape routing logic.
  • Adjusted timing and pacing from playtests.
  • Handled edge cases to prevent softlocks.
Encounters Tuning Gameplay

04

Objective-Driven World State

Doors, level transitions, and encounter beats needed to respond reliably when player objectives were completed.

  • Tracked and validated objective completion.
  • Tied door unlocks and traversal gates to game state.
  • Triggered scene transitions from progression milestones.
  • Added fail-safes to prevent progression softlocks.

Lessons Learned

What The Bookstore taught me.

Horror systems work best when they protect pacing. Looping spaces, enemy timing, audio events, and objective gates all had to support the same feeling of controlled disorientation.
Small-team projects reward flexible ownership. With two developers, I had to move between engineering, design, world building, testing, and audio integration without losing sight of the player experience.
Progression logic needs fail-safes. Objective and transition systems became more reliable when every unlock, door, and scene change had a clear state source.
