03

Lightning Cloud Hazard

The lightning cloud needed to pressure movement without feeling random, so the system focused on telegraphing, strike cadence, and fair gameplay responses.

  • Implemented timed strike cycles, cooldowns, and state handling.
  • Added visible feedback so players could read the danger.
  • Integrated hit detection and hazard responses.
  • Tuned difficulty and timing from playtest feedback.
Timing Feedback Tuning

04

Shifter Enemy Behavior

During polish, enemy behavior had to feel intentional rather than unpredictable, especially when players were already navigating puzzle pressure.

  • Adjusted behavior transitions and detection/response logic.
  • Tuned reactions to reduce confusing or unfair moments.
  • Matched implementation to design goals for encounter difficulty.
  • Fixed behavior edge cases discovered during playtests.

Lessons Learned

What Art House taught me.

Large-team engineering depends on clear communication. Bug fixes and feature changes had to stay aligned with design intent, QA reports, and frequent scene updates.
Polish work is systems work. Small changes to timing, feedback, edge-case handling, and prefab setup had a major effect on how finished the game felt.
Debugging is easier when the team shares a vocabulary. Reproducing issues from playtest notes and Linear tasks forced me to translate player-facing problems into concrete implementation fixes.
