Single-player horror game

Catskill Mine

A first-person horror descent through an unstable mine attraction built in Unity.

I worked as a gameplay engineer and designer, focusing on the systems that make the descent feel dangerous: elevator traversal, enemy encounter behavior, procedural creature motion, cinematic triggers, audio feedback, and player counterplay.

Unity | C# | Perforce | Gameplay Systems | Horror Encounter Design

Main Contributions

  • Engineered elevator traversal with asymmetric acceleration, braking, slip behavior, and layered movement audio to make vertical movement feel unstable and dangerous.
  • Implemented spider encounter behavior including shaft traversal, aggression pacing, flashbang counterplay, and kill-state handling.
  • Built procedural creature motion and VFX using raycast-driven spider leg IK, wall attachment checks, curved line rendering, and grapple/tentacle effects.
  • Created cinematic horror sequences using triggers, camera control, animation/audio events, and post-processing transitions.

Core Systems

01

Elevator Traversal & Tension

The elevator needed to feel heavy, unstable, and unsafe instead of like a normal platform. I tuned the movement around slow acceleration, braking, slip behavior, and audio layers so the descent created pressure by itself.

The goal was to give players control while making every movement decision feel risky inside the mine shaft.

Gameplay Systems Traversal Audio Feedback

02

Spider Encounter System

The enemy system controls shaft traversal, attack pacing, aggression, flashbang reaction, and kill-state logic. I wanted the encounter to feel threatening without becoming unreadable or unfair.

Flashbang counterplay gives the player a limited way to interrupt pressure, which made the encounter less scripted and more interactive.

Enemy AI State Machine Counterplay

03

Procedural Spider IK & Grapple VFX

I used raycasts and wall attachment checks to make spider legs and grapple effects react to the mine geometry instead of feeling pasted on top of the environment.

Curved line rendering and procedural placement helped sell fast creature motion while keeping the player focused on threat direction.

IK VFX Raycasting

04

Cinematic Horror Sequences

I created scripted gameplay moments with triggers, camera control, animation events, audio timing, and post-processing transitions to support horror pacing during key beats.

The challenge was making scenes feel directed while keeping the player oriented and preserving the first-person experience.

Lessons Learned

What Catskill Mine taught me.

Good teamwork turns creative disagreement into a design tool. For a cave cubby-hole uncovering mechanic, we compared a tactile tracing interaction against a clearer key-sequence version using criteria like scope, readability, fantasy fit, and input support. We chose the key sequence, then added animation and effects to keep the physical feel I cared about.
Horror systems depend on timing as much as mechanics. Movement speed, encounter pauses, camera control, audio cues, and post-processing timing all change how fair or frightening the same interaction feels.
Player counterplay makes enemy pressure stronger. The spider felt more engaging once flashbang reactions and attack pacing gave players readable moments to react instead of only waiting for scripted scares.
Procedural animation needs strong constraints. Raycast-driven IK and grapple effects became more convincing when they were tuned around readable contact points rather than unrestricted procedural motion.
