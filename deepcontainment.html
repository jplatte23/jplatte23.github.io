Deep Containment

An SCP-inspired Unity horror game built around procedural spaces, enemy pressure, and underwater traversal.

I built gameplay and technical systems for a 13-week summer project, including procedural hallway generation, A* route validation, enemy archetypes, back-peeking visibility checks, item usage, and water-current movement.

Main Contributions

  • Delivered a solo playable build in 13 weeks by owning end-to-end development through milestone planning, grayboxing, iterative playtests, bug fixing, Git, and in-engine tooling.
  • Built reusable threat behavior by implementing AI for 2 enemy archetypes and ambient fish using finite-state logic, raycasting, and physics queries in C#, supporting encounter checks and environment movement.
  • Ensured procedurally generated levels remained solvable by combining A* pathfinding with modular maze generation, preventing unreachable layouts while preserving varied level designs.
  • Developed interaction and traversal systems including item pickup/usage, back-peeking, and water-current forces.

Core Systems

01

Procedural Hallway Generator

The game needed replayable layouts without creating broken paths, unreadable spaces, or pacing dead zones.

  • Assembled modular hallway tiles into varied layouts.
  • Used layout rules to keep generated spaces readable.
  • Ran seed validation to reject broken routes.
  • Added debug visibility for faster generation tuning.
Procedural Gen Validation Systems

02

A* Solvability Checks

Generated levels had to be validated before play so the player could always reach objectives and key areas.

  • Implemented A* search over generated layout nodes.
  • Detected unsolvable seeds before gameplay began.
  • Supported constraints such as key areas and choke points.
  • Hooked route checks into the generation pipeline.
Algorithms Pathfinding Validation

03

Enemy AI Archetypes

The enemies needed predictable rules that could still create tension inside cramped, generated spaces.

  • Built state-driven patrol, detect, chase, attack, and reset loops.
  • Tuned attack windows, cooldowns, and safe moments.
  • Used raycast/visibility checks for consistent detection.
  • Iterated speeds and spacing to avoid cheap hits.
Enemy AI State Tuning

04

Back-Peeking & Water Interaction

Traversal and threat awareness needed to feel physical, so visibility and water movement both became part of the player’s decision-making.

  • Implemented raycast-driven peeking and visibility gating.
  • Added water current forces that affect movement.
  • Built item pickup and usage loops for exploration.
  • Tuned responsiveness through repeated playtests.
Raycasts Movement Interaction

Lessons Learned

What Deep Containment taught me.

Procedural generation needs validation, not just variety. The generator only became useful once route checks could prove that a layout was playable.
Horror pacing depends on constraints. Enemy speeds, visibility checks, hallway density, and safe windows had to work together to make encounters tense without feeling unfair.
Debug views make complex systems faster to tune. Visualizing generated paths and AI behavior made it much easier to find broken seeds and unclear encounter spaces.
