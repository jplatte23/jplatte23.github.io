Wielderless

A 1-4 player co-op action roguelite where players are cursed weapons hunting for temporary bodies to possess.

Risk of Rain meets V Rising, but the player is the weapon. Each run is about crawling through danger in weapon form, reading NPC hosts for compatibility, luring the right body close enough to possess, and surviving when that body eventually breaks.

Gameplay engineer and designer on a 2-person Unreal Engine 5.6 C++ prototype with Steam multiplayer.

Instagram GitHub

Current prototype: weapon movement, scan/lure interaction, charm and possession flow, host combat, and multiplayer testing.

Main Contributions

  • Developed physics-driven weapon movement with visual smoothing, glow buildup, burst feedback, impact audio, Niagara effects, and player-readable recovery states.
  • Built a server-authoritative possession system transferring player control between physics weapon pawns and replicated NPC hosts, handling controller handoff, weapon state, host death, ejection, and recovery.
  • Profiled and optimized large-scale NPC/world simulation, improving fixed-seed captures from 60.41 to 109.91 FPS through tick staggering, host registries, world caches, and component tick management.
  • Developed a Steam multiplayer session subsystem supporting lobby creation/discovery, lobby-code joining, session lifecycle handling, network-failure cleanup, and ready-gated level travel.
  • Built scan, lure, charm, possess, and consume interactions so choosing a host became a readable gameplay decision instead of grabbing the nearest body.
  • Developed host combat feedback including light attacks, combat roll, reticle-directed melee, swing VFX, hit feedback, and combat audio hooks.

Core Loop

Possession is not a side mechanic. It is the survival loop. Weapon form is vulnerable but still playable, hosts are useful but temporary, and the player is constantly deciding when to scout, lure, charm, possess, fight, sacrifice, or retreat.

01Move as a weapon

Use physics-driven movement to reposition while vulnerable.

02Scan hosts

Reveal a 1-100% compatibility roll before committing to a target.

03Lure and weaken

Tether a target, drain it toward the 30% HP weakened threshold, then choose possess or consume.

04Fight as the host

Use the temporary body for light attacks, combat roll, and reticle-directed melee.

05Return to weapon form

Host death or sacrifice sends the player back into the recovery loop.

Host Choice Replaces Loot

In most roguelites, players look for a better weapon. In Wielderless, the player is the weapon, so the core decision becomes finding a better body. Compatibility is stored on hosts, revealed through scan/lure feedback, and currently scales possessed host health, damage, and movement speed.

Core Systems

01

Weapon Form Movement

Weapon form needed to feel vulnerable but alive, not like a passive downed state. I built impulse-driven movement with jump/burst feedback, metal impact audio, weapon-form UI, and Niagara VFX so players still have agency while searching for a host.

The main tuning challenge was keeping the physics expressive without making it too chaotic or too easy to control, especially once multiplayer consistency became part of the prototype.

Physics Niagara Game Feel

02

NPC Compatibility & Scan

Without information, possession turns into grabbing the nearest NPC. The scan system gives players a reason to evaluate targets by revealing compatibility before they commit to a body.

Compatibility turns host choice into the prototype's version of loot evaluation: a host archetype and its compatibility roll both affect how useful that body becomes after possession.

NPC Data World UI Player Choice

03

Lure System

Weapon-form players need agency before possession, not just a hope that an NPC wanders close enough. The lure lets the weapon deliberately pull a target into a risky setup phase.

I built projectile/tether behavior, percentage-based tether damage, and a weakened/charmed transition where the host can follow the weapon and unlock possess or consume options.

AI Behavior Interaction Iteration

04

Possession & Host Control

Possession is the moment where the fantasy has to land: the weapon becomes the wielder. I implemented the transition from weapon form into host control, including the weapon flying into the host's hand.

The hard part is that this is not just a visual swap. Movement, camera intent, combat availability, player feedback, and replicated state all change when control moves into a host.

State Change Replication Core Loop

05

Steam Multiplayer & Replicated Flow

Because Wielderless is designed around co-op, I wanted multiplayer pressure on the prototype early. I added Steam sessions, lobby codes, session hosting/joining, direct lobby lookup, and replication for the core possession and combat interactions.

This forced ownership questions early: who owns the form change, which state must replicate, and how much presentation can happen locally without lying about gameplay truth?

Steam Sessions Replication

06

Combat Feedback

Host combat needs to feel like the reward for surviving weapon form. I added host light attack, combat roll, swing arc VFX, blood/hit feedback, combat sounds, reticle-directed melee, and temporary reticle-facing behavior after attacks.

The goal was readability: after a form change, players need clear attack direction, timing, and impact feedback immediately.

Lessons Learned

What Wielderless taught me.

Networking forced me to define ownership boundaries much earlier than expected. A possession prototype touches player control, AI state, camera behavior, combat, and replication. Waiting to think about ownership would have made every later system harder to reason about.
Designing the player fantasy first made later technical decisions easier. Once the fantasy was clear, the engineering questions became sharper: how does a weapon move, how does it choose a host, what does another client need to know, and how does feedback sell the state change?
I underestimated how much feedback communicates gameplay state. Glow buildup, Niagara bursts, world-space compatibility UI, attack arcs, reticle behavior, and audio cues are not polish after the fact. They are how players understand the system.
Fast prototypes still need clear state boundaries. Wielderless is teaching me to prototype quickly without letting every mechanic become tangled with every other mechanic. The next step is separating stabilized systems into cleaner gameplay components.
